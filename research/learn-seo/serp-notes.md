# SERP notes, 27 September 2026

Method: live web search for each query (English results, unpersonalised, run from a UK-hosted session on 27 Sep 2026). For each query the top 9 or 10 organic results were recorded as title and URL; the notes below are read from those titles, domains and a look at the ranking pages' shape. Competition is an inferred judgement from who ranks and how the content is shaped, not a keyword-difficulty number. Nothing here is a volume figure.

## Patterns across the SERPs

1. Photoshop tutorials dominate every effect and technique query. A no-Photoshop, in-browser route with a working example on the page is the consistent gap.
2. Print queries are owned by printers. Their pages are commercial and stop at the numbers. The gap is the design step: what to change in the file and how to check it before sending.
3. Comparison head terms (alternative to X) are listicles from competitors and publishers. Not worth chasing; the long tail (offline, private, no upload, PSD support) is open.
4. "Problem" phrasings are underserved because the SERP drifts to a different intent: "print looks blurry" goes to inkjet troubleshooting, "design looks generic" goes to AI UI. A page that answers the design intent directly has little competition.
5. Small tool sites already run programmatic size pages (A5 in pixels, A4 in pixels). Voidcanvas should answer with one strong reference page and a calculator, not by copying the pattern.
6. Nobody in these SERPs shows a live example. Every result is text and screenshots, or a bare tool with no explanation. Combining the two (explain, then demonstrate on the page) is the differentiator the brief asks for.
